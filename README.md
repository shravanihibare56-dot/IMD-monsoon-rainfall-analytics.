IMD District-wise Monsoon Rainfall Analytics (India)
Interactive Dashboard: View on Tableau Public Tools & Frameworks(https://share.google/KPsLRYRRvon7U3Zli): Tableau, Data Cleaning, Geospatial Mapping, Google PACE Framework Data Source: India Meteorological Department (IMD) — Monsoon Period (01 June – 30 September)
Executive Summary
India's Kharif agricultural cycle depends heavily on timely and evenly distributed monsoon rainfall. Raw India Meteorological Department (IMD) district-level tables are dense and difficult for non-technical policymakers to interpret quickly. Using the Google PACE Framework (Plan, Analyze, Construct, Execute), this project transforms raw IMD precipitation records into an interactive 3-in-1 Tableau dashboard—tracking 671,868 actual rainfall against a 776,875 normal baseline (-12.37% overall monsoon departure)—to help agricultural and drought-management stakeholders identify deficit zones at a glance.
Methodology (PACE Workflow)
1.	Plan: Sourced authentic district- and state-level IMD monsoon rainfall records (01 June – 30 September) to evaluate regional precipitation variance.
2.	Analyze: Cleaned raw meteorological tables, resolved missing/null values, and restructured the schema to accurately map district-level geospatial coordinates.
3.	Construct: Built a 3-in-1 interactive Tableau dashboard featuring:
○	KPI Summary Cards: Period Actual (671,868), Avg. Period Departure (-12.37%), and Period Normal (776,875).
○	District-wise Geospatial Map: Color-coded distribution highlighting surplus, normal, and deficit districts across India.
○	State/UT Comparison Chart: Actual vs. Normal rainfall bar chart with cross-filtering across all states and union territories.
4.	Execute: Refined UI layout using native dashboard titles, custom card borders/shading, and interactive cross-filters for self-service exploration by non-technical users.
Key Insights & Stakeholder Impact
●	Overall Monsoon Deficit (-12.37%): National actual rainfall trailed the normal benchmark (671,868 vs. 776,875), signaling heightened stress for rain-fed Kharif crops such as rice, cotton, and soyabean.
●	Regional Disparities: State-level drill-downs reveal sharp variations between excess/normal belts and severe deficit pockets (e.g., localized dry spells across key agricultural states), underscoring the need for district-level rather than state-average planning.
●	Actionable Recommendations:
○	Drought & Water Conservation: Prioritize micro-irrigation and water-conservation interventions in districts showing persistent negative departures.
○	Crop Insurance & Advisory: Use district-level departure thresholds to accelerate crop insurance support and issue timely sowing/irrigation advisories to farmers.
